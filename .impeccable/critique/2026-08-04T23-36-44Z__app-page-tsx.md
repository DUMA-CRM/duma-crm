## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** Low specificity; category-interchangeable. The coffee vocabulary and cup icon identify the sector, but the page uses the standard SaaS sequence of logo/auth header, pill eyebrow, oversized headline, two CTAs, KPI row, floating dashboard mock, three feature cards, and repeated footer links. Another hospitality or retail product could reuse it with minimal changes.

The page also discards DUMA’s authored “Roast Curve” world. Instead of chart stock, comparative traces, ruled fields, square instrument controls, and data-only colour, it uses radial gradients, blur, a floating panel, 24px corners, filled pills, and bare KPI tiles. The four truthful differences—one system/no add-ons, offline-first reliability, role-shaped workspaces, and group control—are mostly absent.

**Deterministic scan:** The bundled detector returned exit code 0 with `[]`: 0 findings, no rule counts, and no file locations. This is not evidence that the page is strategically or visually correct; the scanner did not flag the semantic conflicts visible against `PRODUCT.md` and `DESIGN.md`. There were no detector false positives.

**Visual overlays:** No reliable user-visible overlay is available. The browser’s evaluation scope rejected mutable preflight injection (`document.title` was getter-only), so `detect.js` was not injected and the detector live server was correctly not started. Desktop and 390×844 viewport inspection served as the fallback signal; the mobile page had no horizontal overflow.

## Overall Impression

The page is legible, compact, and initially confident. Its biggest opportunity is to stop borrowing generic SaaS credibility and make DUMA’s real mechanics—and the Roast Curve interface itself—the proof. Today the page promises a distinctive operating system while visually presenting a replaceable template.

## What’s Working

- **The proposition is immediate.** “Run your entire coffee business from one system” communicates the core promise within seconds.
- **The hierarchy is disciplined.** Desktop and mobile preserve a clear headline → explanation → action sequence with readable contrast.
- **Hero actions are explicit and usable.** Both hero CTAs are text-labeled and measured 44px high on the 390px viewport.

## Cognitive Load

Moderate: 3 checklist failures.

- **Single focus fails:** header “Get Started,” hero “Start Free,” and “View Demo Workspace” compete; two labels point at the same sign-up route.
- **Chunking fails:** the hero paragraph compresses seven modules into one undifferentiated sentence.
- **Progressive disclosure fails:** breadth appears before a sequenced case built from differentiators, operational proof, and action.

Grouping, hierarchy, working-memory support, and the absence of more than four localized choices are solid. The first viewport nevertheless reaches exactly four visible actions—already the practical limit.

## Emotional Journey

Arrival is confident because the headline is clear. Confidence falls during consideration: generic features and invented-looking figures replace demonstrable product mechanics. The emotional valley is the decision point, where “Start Free” implies pricing and availability without terms while “View Demo Workspace” gives no expectation of what happens next. The page ends flat with repeated links rather than a memorable proof moment or reassuring close.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Unsupported proof and availability claims undermine trust

**Why it matters:** DUMA is pre-launch with no customers or public proof. “3 min Average setup time,” “24/7 Team visibility,” “Today at DUMA Soho,” exact live metrics, and “Start Free” read as real-world evidence or availability.

**Fix:** Replace them with demonstrable mechanics. Label any preview as illustrative. Use an honest access CTA unless free self-service is genuinely available.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P1] The page hides the four reasons DUMA deserves consideration

**Why it matters:** “All-in-one” alone is copyable. Offline-first reliability, seven role-shaped workspaces, and group-level control—the least copyable claims—are absent.

**Fix:** Build the persuasion arc from four operational scenes: a connection drop mid-sale, role-specific workspaces, single-site-to-group comparison, and one system replacing the add-on stack.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`

### [P1] The visual language contradicts “The Roast Curve”

**Why it matters:** The product has a rare, specific design system, but the landing page looks like glossy SaaS. Marketing and product feel unrelated.

**Fix:** Turn the hero into an instrument reading: chart-stock lattice, measured trace against reference, ruled fields, mono comparative figures, 2px corners, and achromatic actions. Remove ornamental gradients, blur, floating-card shadow, full-round pills, and bare KPIs.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable bolder`

### [P2] The mobile header collapses into a cramped brand/action strip

**Why it matters:** At 390px, “Coffee Business OS” wraps into three lines while two 36px-high controls share the row. The first brand impression looks accidental and the controls miss the 44px touch target used lower on the page.

**Fix:** Keep DUMA plus one mobile action, move Sign In to a secondary location, prevent descriptor wrapping, and use a 44px action.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan — confused first-timer:** “Operating layer” and “leadership control” assume category knowledge. “Get Started” and “Start Free” reach the same route but imply different commitments. Pricing, trial, and pre-launch expectations are absent.

**Riley — deliberate stress tester:** The first challenged claims will be “3 min,” “24/7,” “DUMA Soho,” “Live,” and exact KPI values. None has a source, timestamp, reference, or illustrative-data disclosure; “Start Free” invites a test of availability the page does not explain.

**Casey — distracted mobile user:** The 390px header forces the descriptor into three lines and uses two 36px-high actions. The page runs to roughly 1,965px, repeats actions at the end, and offers no interrupted-user reassurance about what starting entails.

**Mina — independent café owner:** Breadth is promised, but the page does not show how one shop benefits on day one without complexity. No open-to-close shift, offline sale, purchasing, rota, or cash-up story replaces the setup-time claim.

**Theo — multi-site operator:** A single “DUMA Soho” mock makes DUMA appear single-site. Cross-site comparison, audit, workspace scoping, and group configuration are invisible.

## Minor Observations

- The full-round eyebrow and “Live” chip conflict with the boxed-annotation vocabulary.
- KPI figures use ordinary semibold sans text rather than Chivo Mono and have no comparison or “No comparison” disclosure.
- The page background suppresses the body lattice, hiding the system’s spatial signature.
- “Customer Memory Built In” does not cover offline resilience, role-shaped apps, or group control.
- Footer Sign In/Get Started/Demo links repeat earlier routes without adding legal, contact, trust, or product context.
- The coffee-cup mark supplies category recognition, but not product specificity.

## Questions to Consider

- If every exact number disappeared because DUMA cannot yet prove it, which product mechanic would become the hero evidence?
- What remains unmistakably DUMA if the coffee cup and the word “coffee” disappear?
- Could the first viewport show an offline order queued safely while today’s shift trace is read against a reference?
- Is “Start Free” factually available today, or should pre-launch access become an honest part of the proposition?
- Why does the landing page promise one system while visually presenting another design system than the product itself?
